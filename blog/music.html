Why I don't listen to music anymore

Published: August 22, 2026

← Back to Blog

Someone recently commented on one of my most recent guitar videos (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/1-U1jIlMFYk): "What kind of songs do you go through learning to come up with a taste so unique"

Now there are a couple of different answers I have to this question, one being that I don't really listen to music anymore. I like to think of it as starving myself of external musical inspiration so that I can come up with ideas organically. However, this approach to creating music is difficult to do correclty as I'm frequently exposed to cool and inspiring music through Instagram Reels and the anime I've been watching. Therefore, a lot of my musical ideas in the videos I make are some concoction of previously played improvisations and unconscious riffs I developed while listening to these unavoidable stimili. I think the best way to explain this is to break down my most recent guitar video. It's a funky sort of tune I developed that draws inspiration from anime intro music, bossa nova chords, blues licks, original mannerisms, and fusion chords that I sat down and chained together over the course of a day. For me, the discovery process of making a 30 second video can take minutes to hours, it depends on what I'm thinking about and how I'm adapting it to be played on the instrument in front of me. The first lick of the video (B C# E F# G# into a cool Amaj7 voicing I discovered one day) is a cool pentatonic lick that you can find everywhere. The following chord progression draws heavily from Japanese anime chords and has that characteristic descending min7 progression (C#min7 - Cmin7 - Bmin7) you can find in "Just the Two of Us". Stringing together these ideas is a matter of personal taste and I rarely have trouble deciding how to architect my tunes. For example, recently I've gravitated towards resolving my guitar tunes onto E because I have the most vocabulary and prebuilt riffs that can lead to E in an interesting way. Some new voicings I used in this video came from another Instagram Reel a friend shared with me that I then adopted and modified to sound the way I wanted. Another thing I realized when coming up with this tune was that I switch between swinging and playing straight. Playing in time has always been a slight weakness of mine and when I get to come up with solo guitar tunes, I've noticed how I take more liberties with time as I can stretch the time to fit what I'm playing. For example, I spent a bit of time today trying to make this lick I came up with on saxophone sound good on guitar. I ended up keeping a version of the lick that was slurred and the whole section sounds a little bit rushed if you listen closely. It was this idea E E F# G# A C# E B but I added some passing chords to make it sound cool. Later on in the tune I threw in this cool min 11 lick that I've been hearing a lot on Reels and modern jazz piano. It's a variation of the min 11 arpeggio thatcan be heard in this viral TikTok audio by Dorian Concept called "Hide".

A lot of my proficiency in the guitar comes from my background as a classical guitar player. I started taking lessons when I was six and have been playing frequently since then. That's also why guitar is one of the easier instruments for me to record. My technique and fundamentals make playing the instrument incredibly satisfying. Plucking feels easy and lush. On guitar, I'm able to discover ideas faster than on any other instrument I play because I have such a long history with it.

So while there is no good answer to the commenter's question, I can provide some pieces I've learned on classical guitar that heavily influence my taste and current improvisation meta. For one, I learned a couple pieces by Luiz Bonfa on classical guitar, my favorites being "Tenderly" (Bonfa's interpretation of the standard) and "Walking in Rio", two pieces that I have kept in my fingers from when I first learned them two years ago. As for more classical repertoire, if you go to my YouTube channels and look for my oldest videos, you'll find a couple classic classical guitar pieces I learned in my earlier years.

My approach to creating new music isn't straightforward and there is no way to tell exactly where I draw inspiration from. However, I hope this post gives some insight into my creative process.
