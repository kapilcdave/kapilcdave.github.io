A quick sanit(ar)y check

If I drop a bar of soap on the ground is the ground clean and the soap dirty or is the ground dirty and the soap clean?

There may be an obvious answer to this because the ground will always be dirty and the soap will have dirt on it. And from there we can say there are more layers of dirt on the ground than there are layers of soap in the bar of soap, so therefore the ground is still dirty just not as dirty as when the soap was dropped on it. And then the soap is dirty on one layer and is not as clean as it was.

So in that transaction the ground becomes a little less dirty and the soap becomes a little less clean.

But what if water is added?

When someone washes their hand, they require soap and water. Then the hand transfers a layer of dirt to the bar of soap and the water washes the soap away. Then the hand becomes clean, the soap becomes a little less clean, and the water becomes dirty.

If we apply this logic to adding water to the ground in which the soap has fallen, then the ground transfers the dirt to the water and the ground becomes clean!

It seems as if water is able to increase the amount of layers of dirt the soap can penetrate. The soap's power is increased.

But what happens to the soap? Since water is the most granular of these three things, the water eventually slips between the soap and the ground and the soap strips more of its layers when in contact with the water.

Its important to note the soap doesn't get less clean the less amount of layers it has.
